# Tab: the Outro's riff and solo, bars 154–169 (Sweet Child O' Mine, round 9)

Jeremy, 2026-10-28: the ninth round of drawing Guitar Pro's "Sweet Child O' Mine" near identically
(see `2026-10-28-tab-sweet-child-intro-design.md` for round 1 and the rules on copyrighted music, and
`2026-10-28-tab-sweet-child-solo-six-design.md` for round 8). This round is page 9 of the book, bars
154–169: the end of the Outro's riff, a run of hammer-ons getting louder, an artificial harmonic, and
the start of the outro solo, an octave up under the wah.

## What the book prints that Frets didn't

- **A dead note slides into the next.** Bar 160 ends on a dead note slid up into the 19 that opens bar
  161. Frets left the slide out with a warning, as if it had no note to go to. A slide (sl, ss) may
  now start from a dead note; a hammer-on or pull-off still needs two frets. It is drawn as any slide,
  from below.

- **A bend let down part of the way.** In bar 165 a full bend is let down to a half step ("½"), then
  pushed back up to "full", then let down. Frets read `b (4 2)` as let all the way down and wrote it
  back as `b (4 0)`. Now a release keeps the height it stops at (`Bend.to`) and is written back as it
  was. On the tab its arrow lands a step under the bend's line, labelled "½" by its arrowhead; a tied
  note after it that pushes the bend back up rises straight from there, with no dashed line, to the
  first line, as the book draws it. A note holding the bend where it landed draws its dashed line a
  step under. A picked note may be let down part of the way too: `b (0 4 2)`.

- **A crescendo hairpin.** Bar 157's sextuplets get louder under a hairpin opening to the right. Frets
  kept alphaTex `cre` and `dec` as text it doesn't draw. Now a column getting louder or softer
  (`Column.hairpin`) is read and written back, and neighbouring columns that share it draw one wedge,
  in a row of its own right under the staff (over the tab's other marks when the tab is alone).
  Playback ignores it, as it does the wah.

- **An artificial harmonic.** Bar 158 is a squeal: 8 on the B string as an artificial harmonic, under
  "AH", with a natural harmonic at the 7th fret of the G string. Frets kept `ah` as text it doesn't
  draw. Now it is a third kind of harmonic, labelled "AH" in the row that has "P.H.", its touch point
  kept when given (`ah 12`), and the N key's cycle goes natural, pinch, artificial, none. The book also
  draws a diamond head on the staff at the harmonic's pitch; Frets draws the fretted note's head.

Everything else on the page Frets already draws: pull-offs down the strings and a slide (bar 155),
the sextuplets, a whole rest under a chord name, a bend held over the bar line (bars 161–162), pre-bends
with accents and staccato under the octave line, an octave line broken and started again (bar 162),
quintuplets (bars 167–168), bends let down onto a pull-off, and the wah's signs throughout.
