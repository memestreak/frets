# Frets: pedagogy research, round 2

2026-10-07. Follows `2026-10-05-brainstorm.md`. Since then the app gained
the Chords section (library with arpeggio toggle, lab), "In one position"
chord shapes in the Scale lab, and the Circle of fifths page with Key and
Mode views (parallel and relative modes). So "parallel modes" from the
first list is done, and "triads on string sets" and "CAGED" are half done
through the shape generator and position stepper. This note looks at what
other tools and the learning-science literature do, and what's still
missing from Frets.

Sizes as before: **S** one PR on existing parts, **M** a new page reusing
parts, **L** a new engine or input model.

## 1. What other tools do that Frets doesn't

| Tool | The idea worth borrowing | Frets today |
|---|---|---|
| **Hooktheory** (TheoryTab, Trends) | Chords colour-coded by scale degree across every song and key, so the same progression looks the same everywhere; a "what chord usually comes next" tool with probabilities from a corpus of real songs. | Degree colours already exist (`DEGREES` in the fretboard theme). No progressions. |
| **iReal Pro** | Chord changes for thousands of tunes, played back at a chosen tempo and style; "practice" mode transposes a tune through all 12 keys. | No playback, no progressions. |
| **SØLO** (Premier Guitar's list) | Maps chord and scale tones over a progression as "challenges": play through the changes, see chord tones light up as the chord changes. | Arpeggio toggle shows one chord's tones statically. |
| **Functional Ear Trainer** | Plays a cadence to establish a key, then a note; you name its scale degree. Teaches hearing *function*, not intervals. | No audio. The Interval trainer tests eyes and names, not ears. |
| **boardr**, **Fretboard Master** | Microphone pitch detection (the `pitchy` library): the app hears the note you play and advances. | Trainers are tap-only. |
| **Tonnetz apps** (Touch Tonnetz, jazz-library.com) | A triangle lattice where common tones are shared points: adjacent triangles share two notes, so chord motion by common tone is a visible step. | The Clock view shows one chord; nothing shows two chords' overlap. |
| **Negative Harmony Simplified** | Reflect a chord around the key's tonic–dominant axis. | Nothing. Niche, but the Clock could do it in a few lines. |
| **Humanome** | Rhythm app that trains the internal clock: tempo drops out and you keep time. | Nothing. |

Sources: [Hooktheory free resources](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/free-hooktheory-resources/),
[Premier Guitar best apps](https://www.premierguitar.com/pro-advice/tools-for-the-task/best-apps-for-musicians),
[boardr README](https://github.com/VannRR/boardr/blob/main/README.md),
[Tonnetz article](https://jazz-library.com/articles/tonnetz/),

## 2. What the learning literature says

There is little peer-reviewed research specifically on fretboard learning
(the Erudite Guitarist review says so itself), but three general findings
apply directly to Frets' trainers:

- **Interleaving beats blocking** for retention. Carter & Grahn (2016,
  *Frontiers in Psychology*) had clarinettists practise passages blocked
  or interleaved; interleaved practice produced better retention a day
  later even though it felt harder. Frets' trainers currently block: one
  trainer, one settings profile, one session. Mixing question types
  (interval, note, chord tone) in one session would be the app's version
  of this.
- **Retrieval practice with feedback** beats re-reading. The trainers
  already do this well. The gap is that nothing is *scheduled*: the
  generator asks everything equally often, so a learner who knows the
  low strings cold and not the B string gets the same mix forever.
  Spaced repetition (SM-2 or FSRS, both tiny) would fix that with no new UI.
- **Chunking the neck** into small cells (about four frets) and
  **intervallic thinking** (distance from a reference note) are the
  strategies the fretboard-specific literature recommends. Frets already
  does both (the trainers' range boxes, the Scale lab position stepper).
  Worth keeping as the organising principle for anything new.

Sources: [Science of fretboard learning](https://theeruditeguitarist.substack.com/p/the-science-of-fretboard-learning),
[Carter & Grahn 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01251/text),
[retrievalpractice.org on interleaving](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/strategies/2019/4/3/interleaving).

## 3. New ideas, grounded in the above

### 3a. Hear it: audio and ear training

Still the biggest gap, and now the most-cited one in this research.

- **Playback engine** (M, unchanged from round 1). Play a note, strum a
  shape, arpeggiate a chord, loop a progression at a tempo. Web Audio
  with a Karplus–Strong pluck is a few hundred lines and no download;
  samples sound better but cost weight.
- **Functional ear trainer** (M after audio). Play a I–IV–V–I cadence in
  a key, then one note; the learner names its degree *or finds it on the
  neck within a position*. The second form is the fretboard twist no
  piano app has. Reuses the Note trainer's Find-it flow.
- **Hear-and-find intervals** (S after audio): the Interval trainer gains
  a mode where the two notes are heard, not shown. Same answer card.
- **Chord quality by ear** (S after audio): hear a shape from the library,
  pick maj / min / dom7 / m7 / dim. Uses the Chord library voicings.

### 3b. Microphone input (L, optional)

Browser pitch detection (autocorrelation via `pitchy`, as boardr does)
lets Find-it trainers accept a *played* note instead of a tap. Honest
caveats from the sources: octave errors on guitar's rich harmonics, the
low E and F are hard to separate with coarse FFT bins, it needs mic
permission, and phones add latency. It works for single notes, not for
chords. I'd treat it as a later experiment, not a dependency.

Source: [Pitch detection with Web Audio](https://alexanderell.is/posts/tuner/).

### 3c. Progressions, with corpus data

- **Progression player** (M after audio, L with analysis). Type or pick
  chords, see numerals in a key, play them back, step through them with
  the neck showing each chord's shape in one position. This is iReal Pro
  plus the neck.
- **"What comes next"** (M, no audio needed). Hooktheory's Trends idea:
  given the last chord in a key, show the common next chords. Frets has
  no song corpus, so the probabilities would be hand-written from
  functional rules (after V, I or vi; after ii, V) or a small public
  table. Could sit on the Circle of fifths page: tap a chord, the likely
  next chords ring.
- **12-key drill** (S once progressions exist): a ii–V–I or I–vi–IV–V
  transposed through the circle, the neck showing each key's shapes in
  the same position. This is the standard jazz-school exercise.

### 3d. Common tones and chord motion

- **Two-chord overlap on the Clock** (S). Select two chords in the Scale
  lab (or a chord and the next one when stepping) and draw both on the
  clock: shared tones filled, moved tones as arrows. This is the Tonnetz
  insight without the lattice. Also the cheapest way to show voice
  leading, secondary dominants (the one raised note) and tritone subs
  (two chords sharing their guide tones).
- **Tonnetz page** (M). A triangle lattice of pitch classes with the
  current scale's chords as triangles. Attractive, but it teaches
  triads, not guitar shapes; I'd rank it below the Clock overlap.
- **Negative harmony on the Clock** (S, niche): reflect the selected
  chord around the tonic–dominant axis. A one-checkbox feature once the
  overlap exists.

### 3e. Practice scheduling

- **Spaced repetition inside the trainers** (M). Store per-item
  accuracy (per interval × position, per note × string) and bias the
  generator toward weak items. The generators already enumerate every
  valid question, so weighting them is a small change to one function.
  Stats UI could show a per-string heat map on the neck.
- **Mixed session** (M). One Practice page that interleaves intervals,
  notes and chord tones, per the interleaving research. Reuses the
  reducer and answer card; needs the question type in the state.
- **Daily routine** (S): a short fixed session (10 of each) with a
  streak. Cheap engagement; decide whether Frets wants it.

### 3f. Guided paths versus tools

Every app in the survey that reaches beginners (Fender Play, Gibson,
Justin Guitar) is lesson-shaped; everything Frets has is tool-shaped.
A middle path that keeps Frets' character: a **"Learn" column in the
menu** with a few ordered sequences, each step a deep link into an
existing page with its settings preset ("Notes on the E and A strings,
frets 0–5" opens the Note trainer so configured). No prose beyond one
line per step. Size S–M, mostly URL parameters the pages already parse.

## 4. Recommended order, revised

1. **Clock overlap for two chords** (S). Shows voice leading, secondary
   dominants and tritone subs with parts that exist.
2. **Spaced repetition in the trainers** (M). The best-supported idea in
   the literature, invisible UI.
3. **Audio playback** (M). Everything in 3a and 3c waits on it.
4. **Functional ear trainer with find-on-neck** (M). The one ear trainer
   no piano app offers.
5. **Progression player + "what comes next"** (M, then L for analysis).
6. **Mixed session / Learn paths** (M / S–M) once there are enough drills
   to sequence.
7. Microphone input, Tonnetz, negative harmony, rhythm: later experiments.
