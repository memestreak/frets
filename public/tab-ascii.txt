FRETS ASCII TAB
===============

How to write guitar tab that Frets (https://frets.eminor.net/tab) can open
and edit. Written for people and for AI agents turning a picture of tab
into text. Everything below is a rule unless it says "may".

Agents writing tab from a picture: prefer alphaTex
(https://frets.eminor.net/tab-alphatex.txt). It carries more, keeps
everything Frets doesn't draw yet, and nothing in it has to line up.

One voice only: every column has one duration. Riffs, solos, double stops
and strummed chords fit; fingerstyle with a bass line held under moving
notes does not (write the moving part and give held notes as ties).


1. THE WHOLE TEXT
-----------------

    Title: <name of the clip>           optional
    Time: <beats>/<note>                optional; 4/4 if left out
                                        (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 6/8 or 12/8)
    Tuning: <six notes, low to high>    optional; E A D G B E if left out
    <blank line>
    <one or more blocks>

A block is one line of tab across the page ("a system"). Blocks follow one
another in playing order and join into one clip. Put a blank line between
blocks (the last example in section 8 has two). Any other line (chord
names, lyrics, comments) is ignored.

Tuning is recorded and shown with the clip; frets are frets in any tuning.
Without a Tuning line, string names that differ from standard (a "D" on
the bottom line) are read as the tuning. When there is a Tuning line,
the string names may be either the Tuning's notes or standard e B G D A E;
they are not checked against it.


2. A BLOCK
----------

From top to bottom, the first four optional:

    endings line   1st / 2nd endings and repeat counts   (section 7)
    PM line        palm mute                              (section 6)
    strokes line   pick or strum direction                (section 6)
    rhythm line    durations; strongly preferred          (section 4)
    e|...          six string lines, highest string first
    B|...
    G|...
    D|...
    A|...
    E|...

- Exactly six string lines, in this order, nothing between them.
- Each string line starts with its name and "|". The names are labels:
  the first line is always the highest string, the last the lowest.
- All six string lines are the same length. Fill every empty position
  with "-".
- "|" at the same position on all six lines is a bar line. End each line
  with a bar line. A double or thick final bar line in a picture is
  written as a plain "|" too.
- The lines above the strings line up with them character for character,
  counting from the start of the line (so they begin with two spaces,
  under "e|"). Each is told apart by what it holds; keep them in the order
  above.


3. NOTES AND COLUMNS
--------------------

- A note is a fret number, 0 to 24, written on its string's line. "12" is
  one note on two characters.
- A note's position is the character position of its first digit. Notes
  played together (a chord) start at the same position on their strings.
  Everything that starts at one position is one column, played at one
  time.
- "x" in place of a fret is a muted (dead) note.
- "(5)" is a tie: fret 5 on that string keeps sounding from its previous
  note instead of being played again. The position is the digit; the
  "(" sits in the gap before it. Use it for a note or chord held across
  a bar line, or held while other notes move.
- Two notes on one string need at least one other character between them:
  "-" or a technique mark ("5-7", "5h7"). Digits side by side are always
  one fret ("128" is fret 128 and is refused).
- Prefer two characters for each sixteenth note of duration: an eighth
  four characters, a quarter eight. With a rhythm line this spacing is
  only for looks; without one it is how the duration is read (section 5).


4. THE RHYTHM LINE
------------------

The line just above the strings gives each column's duration: a letter
directly above the column's position.

    w  whole      h  half      q  quarter      e  eighth      s  sixteenth

- "." right after the letter makes it dotted ("q." a dotted quarter).
- "3" right after the letter makes it a triplet ("e3": three of them fill
  one quarter). Write triplets in complete groups of three.
- A letter above a position where every string has "-" is a rest of that
  length.
- With a rhythm line, every column needs a letter.
- "|" may be written above bar lines and is ignored. Anything else in the
  line must be a space.
- The durations in each bar must add up to the time signature.

Reading note values from a picture (stems under the tab):

    no stem                        w  whole
    short stem, no flag or beam    h  half
    full stem, no flag or beam     q  quarter
    one flag or beam               e  eighth
    two flags or beams             s  sixteenth
    a dot after the stem           .  dotted
    a "3" over a beamed group      3  triplet

Then count the bar: if the values you read don't add up, look again
before writing anything else.


5. NO RHYTHM LINE
-----------------

When a block has no rhythm line, durations are guessed from spacing: a
column lasts until the next column (or the bar line), and a bar's width
stands for one bar of the time signature; with no bar lines, the most
common gap is taken as an eighth. Guesses snap to the nearest plain or
dotted length, and Frets says how many it guessed.
Leave the rhythm line out only when the rhythm is unknown.


6. TECHNIQUES
-------------

Written on the string line, around the note:

    5h7      hammer-on from 5 to 7
    7p5      pull-off from 7 to 5
    5/7      slide up from 5 to 7
    7\5      slide down from 7 to 5
    /7  \7   slide into 7 from below / above (no earlier note on that
             string)
    7b       quarter-tone bend ("curl")
    7b8      bend 7 up to the pitch of fret 8: a half-step bend (1/2)
    7b9      ... fret 9: a whole-step bend (full)
    7b10     ... fret 10: 1 1/2;  7b11: 2 steps (the most there is)
    7b9r7    bend and release back to 7
    7pb9     pre-bend: bent to the pitch of 9 before it is picked
    7pb9r7   pre-bend and release
    7~       vibrato ("~" right after the note, or after a bend)
    x        muted note
    (5)      tie (section 3)
    <12>     natural harmonic at fret 12
    t12      tap at fret 12

"h", "p", "/" and "\" sit just before a note and join it to the previous
note on the same string, however many "-" lie between them ("5--h7" is
the same as "5h7"). The joined note's position is its first digit, so the
mark sits in the gap before it. "/" or "\" with no earlier note on that
string is a slide in. Likewise "<", "(" and "t" sit in the gap before the
note's first digit.

In "7b9", "7b9r7" and "7pb9r7" the position is the "7"; the rest belongs
to that note and is not a column. "p" followed by a digit is a pull-off;
"pb" is a pre-bend.

Palm mute is a line above the strokes and rhythm lines:

    PM-------|

The run starts at "P" and ends at the last "-" or "|"; every column whose
position falls inside it is palm muted. The run may reach past its last
column, to match where a picture's P.M. line ends; it may not reach a
column that isn't muted. A PM line may hold several runs,
separated by spaces.

Pick or strum direction is a line of "d" (down) and "u" (up), each
directly above a column's position, between the PM line and the rhythm
line. Columns may be left without one.

Other spellings. Tab on the web varies, and Frets also reads these, but
write the forms above:

    5s7      slide (up or down, from the frets); "5^7" hammer or pull
    5h-7     a "-" or two between h, p, /, \, s or ^ and the note
    12*      natural harmonic, as <12>; "[12]" too
    7v       vibrato, as 7~
    0M       palm-muted note
    P.M.- - - -|   palm mute with dots, or with spaced dashes
    n  V     down and up strokes; a stroke one character off still counts
    1| ... 6|      strings numbered from the high e, or not named at
                   all ("|--5--" or "--5--|")

Frets does not read these the way their tabs mean them: a tap written
after its fret ("12t" in "5h8h12t5"), "pb7" with no starting fret, "x5"
for a palm-muted 5, whammy bar marks ("\1/"), trills and tremolo ("tr",
"TP"), accents (">") and labels such as "A.H." or fingering lines
("p i m a"). Translate them before importing.


7. REPEATS AND ENDINGS
----------------------

On all six string lines:

    |:       start of a repeat (":" right after a bar line; at the very
             start of a block, "e|:")
    :|       end of a repeat (":" right before a bar line)
    :|:      end of one repeat and start of the next

Without a start, a repeat goes back to the beginning of the clip. It
plays twice unless the endings line gives a count: "x4", written so that
its last character is right above the end repeat's "|".

Endings go on the endings line, the top line of the block:

    1.-------| 2.-------|

"1." starts the first-time bracket at that position, "-" carries it on,
and it ends at the "|" right above the bar line that closes it (usually
the end repeat). The second ending starts after it. "3." and so on may
follow. The "1." goes above the bar's first column, not above its bar
line. The endings line may also hold a repeat count ("x3"), with at least
one space between it and any bracket.

9. OPENING IT IN FRETS
----------------------

Paste the text into Import on https://frets.eminor.net/tab, or make a
link that opens it directly:

    https://frets.eminor.net/tab#tab=<the whole text, percent-encoded>

Percent-encode as JavaScript's encodeURIComponent does: a line break is
%0A, a space %20, "|" %7C, "/" %2F, "\" %5C, "(" and ")" and "~" are left
as is. Keep the link under about 8,000 characters; split longer tab into
several links. The part after "#" is never sent to a server. Opening it
replaces the clip open in Frets.


10. FROM A PICTURE OF TAB: CHECKLIST FOR AGENTS
-----------------------------------------------

1. Read the title, time signature and tuning. Write "Time:" always;
   write "Title:" and "Tuning:" only when the picture shows them.
2. Copy each system as one block, string by string, top line = highest
   string. Tab in pictures always puts the highest string on top.
3. Line up notes that sound together at one position.
4. If the picture shows rhythm (stems and beams under the tab, or a staff
   above it), write a rhythm line: a letter for every column, rests
   included. Count each bar against the time signature.
5. Translate the marks: arcs with H or P to h/p, slanted lines to / or \,
   curved arrows to b with the target fret from their label (1/4, 1/2,
   full, 1 1/2, 2), a straight-up arrow before picking to pb, wavy lines
   to ~, "P.M." to a PM line, down and up strum signs (arrows, or the
   square and V signs) to a strokes line, parenthesised frets and tie
   arcs to (5), repeat signs and "x4" to |: :| and the count, ending
   brackets to an endings line, "x" to x, diamond notes or <12> to <..>,
   "T" to t. A bend whose arrow starts already up, or is labelled
   "PB" or "pre-bend", is pb, not b. Leave out marks this format has no letter for (let ring,
   tremolo picking, slurs across several notes).
6. Check: six string lines per block, all the same length, every note
   followed by at least one non-digit, bar lines aligned, every column
   with a rhythm letter, each bar adding up.
7. Unless told to give only the text, give the user the link from section 9 and the text, in case the link
   is too long for the chat.
