FRETS ALPHATEX
==============

How to write guitar tab that Frets (https://frets.eminor.net/tab) opens and
draws, as alphaTex. Written for people and for agents turning a picture
of tab into text. Everything below is a rule unless it says "may".

alphaTex is the text notation of alphaTab
(https://alphatab.net/docs/alphatex/introduction). Frets reads it with
alphaTab's own parser (version 1.8.4), so anything alphaTab accepts is read
the same way here; this guide covers the part Frets draws, in the form
Frets writes it. Prefer alphaTex to ASCII tab when writing tab from a
picture: nothing has to line up in columns.

One voice, six strings. Riffs, solos, double stops and strummed chords fit;
fingerstyle with a bass line held under moving notes does not (write the
moving part and give held notes as ties). Several guitar parts may be
written as tracks (see "Tracks" below): Frets keeps them all and shows,
edits and plays one at a time, picked from a list over the clip.


1. THE WHOLE TEXT
-----------------

    // frets:fx <on|off>; distortion <on|off> drive=<0-1> tone=<0-1>; reverb <on|off> amount=<0-1> decay=<0.3-5>
                                      optional; first line. The playback
                                      effects (see 8.); with tracks, one
                                      under each \track line instead.
                                      Other comments are skipped and not
                                      kept
    \title "<name of the clip>"       optional
    \artist "<band or player>"        optional; printed under the title
    \words "<lyricist>"               optional; "Words by ..."
    \music "<composer>"               optional; "Music by ...", or one
                                      "Words & Music by ..." when the same
    \instrument <sound>               optional; acousticguitarsteel (the
                                      default), electricguitarclean or
                                      acousticgrandpiano. Any General MIDI
                                      instrument is kept, and played by the
                                      nearest of these three. With the
                                      piano, write \tuning too, so other
                                      apps read it.
    \ts (<beats> <note>)              optional; (4 4) if left out
                                      (beats 1 to 32; note 2, 4, 8, 16 or 32)
    \tuning (<six notes, HIGH to low, with octaves>)
                                      optional; standard (E4 B3 G3 D3 A2 E2)
    \capo <fret>                      optional; 1 to 12
    \multiBarRest                     optional; bars of rest in a row
                                      are drawn as one, with their count
                                      (always, with several tracks)
    \systemsLayout (<bars> <bars> ...) optional; how many bars each line
                                      holds, first line first
    \defaultSystemsLayout <bars>      optional; bars on each line past
                                      the list (3 when only the list is
                                      written). With neither, lines fill
                                      the width.
    <bars>

End each bar with "|". A line break is just a space: a bar may go on over
several lines and several bars may share one. Frets writes lines of at most
80 characters, as many whole bars to a line as fit and a section on a new
line; a longer bar goes on over indented lines. Drop D is
\tuning (E4 B3 G3 D3 A2 D2). The tuning is printed under the title ("Tune
down 1/2 step" and each string when all six are tuned alike) and played;
frets are frets in any tuning. With all six tuned alike (E-flat tuning is
\tuning (D#4 A#3 F#3 C#3 G#2 D#2)) the staff is written as fingered, as if
in standard tuning, as guitar books print it; otherwise as it sounds.

Tracks. A part for another guitar ("Gtr II") is a track of its own: write
\track "<name>" before each part's lines and bars, the first included,
then that part's effects line (see 8.), \instrument, \tuning and \capo,
then its bars. Lines for
the whole clip (\title, \artist, \words, \music, \multiBarRest, the
layout tags) come before the first \track; \ts follows the first track's
own lines. Every track has the same number of bars. Time and key
signatures, repeats, endings, sections and tempos belong to every track:
write them in the first track's bars only. Each track is one staff of
six strings with one voice. Frets keeps every track and writes them back,
and shows, edits and plays one at a time: the reader picks it from a list
over the clip, by the names \track gives, and a link always opens on the
first. In a clip of several tracks a track's bars of rest in a row are
always drawn as one multi-bar rest with their count, \multiBarRest or
not: write "r.1 |" for each bar a part sits out.

    \title "Two guitars"
    \track "Gtr I"
    \ts (4 4)
    \ro 0.6.8 3.6.8 5.6.8 0.6.8 3.6.8 6.6.8 5.6.8 3.6.8 | \rc 2 0.6.1 |
    \track "Gtr II"
    \tuning (E4 B3 G3 D3 A2 D2)
    (0.4 0.5 0.6).1 | (0.4 0.5 0.6).1 |

With \systemsLayout or \defaultSystemsLayout, and the viewer's View options
set to "Clip's line breaks" (off by default), the lines break as written:
a line too full is squeezed, and wraps only when even that doesn't fit the
screen (the next line in the list starts after it). A multi-bar rest counts
as one bar. Bars are spaced by their notes; \scale and \width are kept but
not used.


2. BEATS AND NOTES
------------------

A bar is beats, separated by spaces. A beat is one note, a chord or a rest,
then "." and its duration:

    5.3.8              fret 5 on string 3, an eighth
    (3.1 0.2 0.3).4    a chord: notes in parentheses, one duration
    r.4                a rest
    x.5.8              a muted (dead) note on string 5
    -.3.4              a tie: string 3 keeps sounding from its last note

- Strings are numbered as in tab: 1 is the high e, 6 the low E.
- Durations: 1 whole, 2 half, 4 quarter, 8 eighth, 16 sixteenth,
  32 thirty-second, 64 sixty-fourth. A 64 takes no dots.
- Write the duration on every beat. (alphaTab carries a duration over to
  the beats after it, so a missing one is not an error, but it is easy to
  get wrong.)
- Note effects go in braces right after the note, before the duration:
  5.3{h}.8. Beat effects go in braces after the duration: 5.3.8{sd}.
  Several effects share one pair of braces, separated by spaces, in any
  order: {h v}, {b (0 4) v}.
- The durations in each bar must add up to the time signature. Grace
  notes (section 4) take no time and don't count.


3. NOTE EFFECTS FRETS DRAWS
---------------------------

    h          hammer-on or pull-off TO THE NEXT NOTE on this string. Put
               it on the first note: 5.3{h}.8 7.3.8 is 5 hammered to 7;
               7.3{h}.8 5.3.8 is 7 pulled off to 5. There is no "p":
               pull-offs are h too.
    sl         slide TO THE NEXT NOTE on this string, on the first note:
               5.3{sl}.8 7.3.8 slides 5 up to 7. A dead note may slide
               up into the next: x.3{sl}.8 19.3.8.
    ss         shift slide to the next note (the second note is picked
               again); written like sl.
    sib  sia   slide into this note from below / from above, from no
               fret in particular.
    b (..)     bend, as points in quarter-tones, evenly spaced:
                 b (0 2)      half-step bend (½)
                 b (0 4)      full bend
                 b (0 4 0)    full bend and release
                 b (4 4)      pre-bend (bent before picking)
                 b (4 4 0)    pre-bend and release
               The bent amount is 1 (¼), 2 (½), 4 (full), 6 (1½) or 8 (2);
               it sets every point that isn't 0, so "PB ½" is b (2 2).
               A bend held over tied notes carries on on each tie:
                 -.3{b (4 4)} holds it (drawn as a dashed line),
                 -.3{b (4 0)} lets it down (an arrow down, no number),
                 -.3{b (4 6)} pushes it on up, full to 1½ (an arrow up
                 from the dashed line, no number),
                 -.3{b (4 2)} lets it down only to ½ (labelled ½ where
                 it lands); -.3{b (2 4)} after it pushes it back up.
               A bend let down part of the way on its own note: b (0 4 2).
               A grace note tied into a bend: 17.1.8{gr} -.1{b (0 4)}.2
    sod  sou   slide out of this note, down / up, to no fret in particular
    v          vibrato
    vw         wide vibrato
    nh         natural harmonic: 12.3{nh}
    ph         pinch harmonic ("P.H." over it): 3.3{ph}
    ah         artificial harmonic ("AH" over it): 8.2{ah}; ah 12 says
               where it is touched, in frets above the fretted note.
    pm         palm mute, on each muted note
    lr         let ring, on each note left to ring
    g          ghost note, drawn in brackets: 5.3{g}
    ac  hac    accent (>) / heavy accent (^)
    st         staccato (a dot over the note)
    umordent   mordent: the note, the one above, and back: 7.3{umordent}
    lmordent   lower mordent: the note, the one below, and back


4. BEAT EFFECTS FRETS DRAWS
---------------------------

    d          dotted: 5.3.4{d} is a dotted quarter
    dd         double dotted
    tu 3       triplet; put it on each of the three: 7.3.8{tu 3}
    tu 5       quintuplet: five in the time of four, on each of the five
    tu 6       sextuplet: six in the time of four
    tu 7       septuplet: seven in the time of four. Five, six or seven
               sixteenths with {tu n} fill one quarter.
    tu 9       nine in the time of eight; tu 10, tu 11 and tu 12 the same:
               nine to twelve thirty-seconds with {tu n} fill one quarter.
    tu (n d)   n in the time of d, for any other ratio of up to twelve
               notes: 5.3.8{tu (3 4)} is three eighths filling a half.
    gr         grace note, drawn small, played just before the next beat
               and taking no time in the bar: 5.3{h}.8{gr} 7.3.4 is a
               grace 5 hammered to 7. Write one grace note as .8, two in
               a row as .16, three or more as .32 (alphaTab reads them
               so whatever is written). "gr ob" is played on the beat.
    tp 8       tremolo picking, as fast as eighths: one slash across the
    tp 16      stem; two slashes tp 16, three tp 32. The beat's own
    tp 32      duration is how long it is picked: 12.1.2{tp 16}.
    sd  su     pick or strum stroke down / up
    wahc waho  the wah pedal closed (+) or open (o) from this beat, drawn
               over the music: 10.2.8{wahc} 8.1.16{waho}. Playback ignores it.
    cre  dec   getting louder / softer from this beat: neighbouring beats
               that have it draw one hairpin, under the staff and over the
               tab: 7.3.16{cre} 9.3.16{cre}. Playback ignores it.
    fermata medium  a fermata over the beat (fermata short, fermata long
               for the other shapes): 0.6.1{fermata medium}. Over the
               staff, over the tab without one. Playback ignores it.
    tb (0 -24) the whammy bar over the beat's length, in quarter tones
               down from the note, spread evenly over it: 4 a whole step,
               24 an octave. tb (0 -24) dives, tb (0 -2 0) dips,
               tb (-6 -6) is pre-dipped and held, tb (-14 0) returns.
               Drawn from the number down under the tab, labelled in steps
               (-6), under "w/ bar". A tied note held down takes the same
               depth twice: 15.2.2{tb (0 -24)} -.2.8{tb (-24 -24)}.
    tt         tapped (T)
    ot 8va     under an octave line over the staff: the notes sound as
    ot 8vb     the tab says and are written an octave (15ma, 15mb: two)
    ot 15ma    lower or higher. Put it on every beat under the line:
    ot 15mb    12.1.4{ot 8va} 14.1.4{ot 8va}. The tab and playback
               ignore it.
    ch "Am7"   a chord name over the beat, as written: 0.1.2{ch "C/G"}.
               Put it on the beat it is written over; a chord name may
               be anything ("N.C.", "Am7(b5)").
    txt "..."  free text over the beat, drawn in italics from that beat
               rightward: 0.1.1{txt "let ring, slowing"}. Write words
               printed over the tab that aren't a chord name, section or
               tempo ("rit.", "harm.", "fade out") this way.


5. REPEATS AND ENDINGS
----------------------

Put these at the start of a bar, before its beats:

    \ts (3 4)                  a new time signature from this bar on;
                               the one in the header holds until then
    \section "Verse"           a section starts here, its name in bold;
    \section ("A" "Chorus")    with a rehearsal mark, "A", in a box
    \tempo 90                  the tempo from this bar on, in bpm
    \tempo (90 "Slowly")       (20 to 400), with a label; the first
                               bar's is printed at the top, under
                               the tuning (120 when it has none)
    \ks G                      the key signature from this bar on; the
                               staff spells its notes in it
    \ro        the bar starts a repeat
    \rc 2      the bar ends a repeat played twice (\rc 4: four times)
    \ae 1      the bar is under the 1st-time ending (\ae 2: 2nd ending)
    \db       the bar ends with a double bar line (a section's end)
    \ft       the bar is in free time: its closing line is dashed, and
               the first of a run says "Free time" with the time
               signature in brackets. Give every free bar its own \ft.

Without \ro, a repeat goes back to the beginning. A bar that both starts
and ends a repeat takes both: \ro \rc 3. With 1st and 2nd endings, the
last bar under the "1." bracket carries the \rc; the "2." bars don't.


6. EVERYTHING ELSE IS KEPT
--------------------------

Any other alphaTex is kept exactly as written and written back out, though
Frets doesn't draw it yet, and Frets lists it under the clip. Write these
when the picture shows them:

    note effects   tr (9 16) trill, ten tenuto
    beat effects   gr b bend grace note, tb (0 4) a whammy pull-up (above
                   the note), tb dive (0 -4) with a type or style
    bar tags       \jump Fine

Not read at all (an error): more than one staff in a track, or more than
one voice; a tuning of other than six strings. Read with a warning: other time signatures (as
4/4, or in a later bar as no change), notes shorter than a sixty-fourth
(as sixty-fourths). Kept as written, as plain notes: tuplets of more
than twelve notes (tu (13 8)), tremolo picking with four or more slashes.


7. EXAMPLES
-----------

Each reads in Frets with no warnings and writes back exactly as shown.

    \title "Riff"
    \ts (4 4)
    5.5{h}.8 7.5.8 5.4{h}.8 7.4.8 7.3{h}.8 5.3.8 7.4.4 |
    5.3{sl}.8 7.3.8 8.2.4 r.4 0.6.4 |

    \title "Bends"
    \ts (4 4)
    7.3{b (0 4)}.4 8.2{b (0 2)}.4 9.3{b (0 4 0)}.2 |
    10.2{b (4 4 0)}.4{d} 8.1.8 10.2{v}.2 |

    \title "Strum"
    \ts (4 4)
    (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).4{sd} (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).8{sd}
      (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).8{su} (0.1 1.2 0.3 2.4 3.5).4{sd}
      (0.1 1.2 0.3 2.4 3.5).4{sd} |
    (-.1 -.2 -.3 -.4 -.5).2 0.6{pm}.8 0.6{pm}.8 0.6{pm}.8 0.6{pm}.8 |

    \title "Repeats"
    \ts (4 4)
    \ro 0.6.8 0.6.8 3.6.8 0.6.8 2.5.4 0.5.4 | \ae 1 \rc 2 3.6.2 5.6.2 |
    \ae 2 (0.1 0.2 1.3 2.4 2.5 0.6).1 | \ro \rc 3 2.4.4 4.4.4 5.4.4 4.4.4 |

    \title "Drop D"
    \ts (6 8)
    \tuning (E4 B3 G3 D3 A2 D2)
    0.6{pm}.8 0.6{pm}.8 (x.4 x.5 x.6).8 (5.4 5.5 5.6).8 (5.4 5.5 5.6).8 0.6.8 |
    (3.4 3.5 3.6).4{d} (0.4 0.5 0.6).4{d} |

    \title "Funk"
    \ts (4 4)
    0.6{ac}.8 0.6{g}.16 0.6{g}.16 3.6{st}.8 3.6{st}.8 5.5{hac}.4 x.5{g}.8
      5.5{ac}.8 |
    0.5{lr}.8 2.4{lr}.8 2.3{lr}.8 1.2.8 3.3{vw ph}.4 5.2{sod}.4 |

    \title "Song"
    \ts (4 4)
    \capo 2
    \section "Verse" \tempo 92 (0.1 1.2 0.3 2.4 3.5).2{ch "C" sd}
      (0.1 1.2 2.3 2.4 0.5).2{ch "Am" sd} |
    \section ("A" "Chorus") (1.1 1.2 2.3 3.4 3.5 1.6).2{ch "F" sd}
      (3.1 0.2 0.3 0.4 2.5 3.6).2{ch "G" sd} |

    \title "Fast"
    \ts (3 4)
    5.2.16 8.2.16 5.1.16 8.1.16 7.3.8{tu 3} 5.3.8{tu 3} 7.3.8{tu 3} 12.1.4{tt} |
    (12.3{nh} 12.4{nh}).8 r.8 9.3.2 |

    \title "Shred"
    \ts (4 4)
    7.3{h}.8{gr} 9.3.4 12.2.16{tu 5} 10.2.16{tu 5} 8.2.16{tu 5} 10.2.16{tu 5}
      12.2.16{tu 5} 12.1.2{tp 16} |
    \ts (3 4) 5.4{sl}.8{gr} 7.4.4 5.4.4 7.4.4 |

    \title "Dive"
    \ts (4 4)
    12.2.4{tb (0 -2 0)} 10.2.4 12.2.2{tb (0 -8 0)} |
    0.6.2{tb (0 -24)} -.6.4{tb (-24 -24)} r.4 |

    \title "Two guitars"
    \track "Gtr I"
    \instrument electricguitarclean
    \ts (4 4)
    \section "Riff" \ro 0.6.8 3.6.8 5.6.8 0.6.8 3.6.8 6.6.8 5.6.8 3.6.8 |
    \rc 2 0.6.8 3.6.8 5.6.8 3.6.8 0.6.8 5.5.8 3.6.8 0.6.8 |
    \track "Gtr II"
    \instrument acousticguitarsteel
    r.1 | (2.4 2.5 0.6).1{ch "Em"} |


8. OPENING IT IN FRETS
----------------------

Paste the text into Import on https://frets.eminor.net/tab, paste it
anywhere on that page, or make a link that opens it directly:

    https://frets.eminor.net/tab#tab=<the whole text, percent-encoded>

Percent-encode as JavaScript's encodeURIComponent does: a line break is
%0A, a space %20, "\" %5C, "{" %7B, "}" %7D, "|" %7C, "(" and ")" are left
as is. Keep the link under about 8,000 characters. The part after "#" is
never sent to a server. Opening it shows the tab as a new arrangement, not
saved until the person presses Save (it asks first if they have unsaved
changes). If the text can't be read, Frets says why, with the line and column.

When you can run code, make a shorter link: compress the text and put it
after #tabz= instead. Take the text as UTF-8, deflate it raw (no zlib
header or checksum), and write the bytes as base64url ("-" and "_" for "+"
and "/") with no "=" padding:

    https://frets.eminor.net/tab#tabz=<the compressed text>

It is two to four times shorter than #tab= for a short clip, and up to
seven times for a whole song. In Python:

    import base64, zlib
    deflate = zlib.compressobj(9, zlib.DEFLATED, -15)
    data = deflate.compress(text.encode()) + deflate.flush()
    link = 'https://frets.eminor.net/tab#tabz=' + base64.urlsafe_b64encode(data).decode().rstrip('=')

In Node.js:

    const link = 'https://frets.eminor.net/tab#tabz=' + zlib.deflateRawSync(text).toString('base64url');

Chat apps cut long links: Discord at 2,000 characters. A compressed link
holds about 23 characters a bar of riffs and 60 of a busy solo. When the
link would be longer than the chat takes, or than about 8,000 characters
anywhere, give the text and say to paste it into Import.

The sound it plays in is the text's \instrument (see 1.), and the
playback effects belong to the text, as one comment line at its top:

    // frets:fx on; distortion on drive=0.5 tone=0.5; reverb on amount=0.3 decay=1.5

Each track has its own effects: with tracks, write each track's line on
the line after its \track. A line before the second \track belongs to the
first track. The metronome's clicks never go through the effects.

The first word turns the effects on or off together; distortion and
reverb are each on or off; drive, tone and amount run 0 to 1, decay 0.3 to
5 seconds. A part or setting left out takes its default (effects off;
distortion on, drive 0.5, tone 0.5; reverb on, amount 0.3, decay 1.5).
Without the line the effects are off.

The same link and Import also take ASCII tab
(https://frets.eminor.net/tab-ascii.txt); Frets tells the two apart by
the text: ASCII tab has runs of dashes, alphaTex never does.


9. FROM A PICTURE OF TAB: CHECKLIST FOR AGENTS
----------------------------------------------

1. Header: write \ts always; \title, \artist, \words, \music, \tuning and
   \capo only when the picture shows them ("Capo 3rd fret" is \capo 3);
   \systemsLayout with each line's bar count to keep the picture's lines;
   \multiBarRest when a bar shows a thick bar across the staff with a
   number over it, which stands for that many bars of "r.1 |". Parts for
   two or more guitars (Gtr I, Gtr II) are tracks, each after its own
   \track "<name>", with the same number of bars. A tuning like "D A D G B E" (low to high) is written high
   to low with octaves: \tuning (E4 B3 G3 D3 A2 D2). Give each string the
   octave nearest its standard note (E4 B3 G3 D3 A2 E2): DADGAD is
   \tuning (D4 A3 G3 D3 A2 D2).
2. Read the strings: the top line of tab is string 1 (high e), the bottom
   line string 6.
3. Read each beat's length from the stems under the tab:

       no stem                        1  whole
       short stem, no flag or beam    2  half
       full stem, no flag or beam     4  quarter
       one flag or beam               8  eighth
       two flags or beams             16 sixteenth
       three flags or beams           32 thirty-second
       four flags or beams            64 sixty-fourth
       a dot after the stem           {d}
       a "3" over a beamed group      {tu 3} on each of the three
       a 5, 6 or 7 over a group       {tu 5}, {tu 6}, {tu 7} on each
       a small fret number before     {gr} on it, written .8 (.16 for
         a full-size one              two, .32 for three or more)
       slashes across a stem          {tp 8}, {tp 16}, {tp 32} for one,
                                      two or three

   A stem belongs to its beat even when the frets are in parentheses (a
   tie or a ghost note). Rests sit under the tab with no fret above them:

       a small block or bar           1 or 2 (a whole or half rest)
       a zigzag                       r.4
       a dot with a slanted stroke    r.8  (two dots: r.16, three r.32,
                                      four r.64)

   Then count each bar against the time signature; if it doesn't add up,
   look again: the bar's total is the surest guide to a short stem. A
   time signature in the middle of the tab, after a bar line, is \ts
   at the start of that bar; count the bars after it against it.
4. Translate the marks: an arc with H or P to h ON THE FIRST NOTE; a
   slanted line between notes to sl on the first note, one into a note
   to sib or sia; a curved arrow to b with points from its label (1/4,
   1/2, full, 1 1/2, 2 as 1, 2, 4, 6, 8; an unlabelled one is full); a
   straight arrow already up before picking, or a "PB" label, to a
   pre-bend b (4 4); an arrow back down after a bend or pre-bend adds a
   release, a last point 0: b (0 4 0), b (4 4 0); a bend whose dashed
   line runs on over tied notes in brackets to b (4 4) on each held tie
   and b (4 0) on the tie its arrow comes down on (that note's number is
   often not printed; it is still a tie, "-"); a wavy line to v, a
   wide or heavy one to vw; "P.M." to pm and "let ring" (or "l.r.") to lr
   on every note from where its line starts to its end tick, muted (x)
   notes included; "P.H." or "PH" to ph; strum signs to sd / su (the
   square or a down arrow is down, the V or an up arrow is up); a "+"
   over a beat to wahc and an "o" to waho; a line from a fret down under
   the tab to a number in steps ("-6", "-1 1/2"), often under "w/ bar",
   to tb on that beat, four quarter tones a step: a slanted line from the
   fret tb (0 -24), a V tb (0 -2 0), a dashed drop straight down first
   (pre-dipped) tb (-6 -6), a line back up to the next fret tb (-14 0) on
   that tied note ("w/ bar" itself is not written); a fret
   joined by a tie arc to the same fret before it to "-" in place of the
   fret (a tied fret is often also in parentheses); a fret in parentheses
   with no tie arc to g; ">" over a note to ac, "^" to hac, a dot over it
   to st; a short slanted line after a note leading nowhere to sod (down)
   or sou (up); a repeat's thick-thin bar with
   dots to \ro (dots after it) or \rc (dots before it), with n from an
   "xn" over it, else 2; ending brackets to \ae n on every bar under
   them; "x" to x in place of the fret; diamond notes or <12> to nh; "T"
   to tt on the note under it; a chord name over the tab to ch "..." on
   the beat under it; a boxed word over a bar ("Verse", "Chorus") to
   \section, and a letter in a box to its rehearsal mark; "♩ = 90" over a
   bar to \tempo 90 (a word before it, "Slowly ♩ = 60", to
   \tempo (60 "Slowly")); other words over the tab ("rit.", "harm.")
   to txt "..." on the beat they start over. Marks from section 6 are written as shown
   there.
5. A thick final bar line is just the last "|"; a bar ending in two thin
   lines starts with \db.
6. Check: every beat has fret.string.duration, strings 1 to 6, frets 0 to
   24, each bar adds up, every bar ends with "|".
7. Unless told to give only the text, give the user the link from section
   8 (#tabz= when you can run code) and the text, in case the link is too
   long for the chat.


10. LESSONS FROM TRANSCRIBING BOOKS
-----------------------------------

- Spell in the key. Put the piece's key signature in \ks at the first
  bar, and again wherever it changes; Frets spells the staff from it.
  Don't copy odd spellings from a Guitar Pro score (a C-flat in G major is
  Guitar Pro's, not the music's).
- Tuned-down guitars: write the real \tuning and keep the frets as
  printed (see 1.). Frets prints "Tune down 1/2 step" and writes the staff
  as fingered, as books do, while playback sounds at the real pitch.
- Legato runs: an arc over several notes is h (or sl for a slide) on
  every note under it but the last:
      14.2{h}.16 15.2{h}.16 14.2{sl}.16 12.2.16
  A hammer-on from nowhere (no picked note before it) is lht.
- A grace note tied into a bend:
      17.1.8{gr} -.1{b (0 4) v}.2 -.1{b (4 4) v}.4 -.1{b (4 0)}.8
  A held bend pushed further up is -.1{b (4 6)}, let down part way
  -.1{b (4 2)}. "1/2" then "full" on one note is b (0 2), then a tie with
  b (2 4). Slides into bends and vibrato on bends: write what is printed,
  12.3{sl}.16 14.3{sl}.16 12.3{b (0 2)}.16 or {b (0 4) v}.
- Several guitar parts: Frets holds one. Write the part the user names; if
  they don't say, take the top (lead) part and say so. Never merge two
  parts into one line.
- "Free time" over a bar is \ft on that bar (and on every free bar after
  it); other words over the tab are txt.
- Say in one line anything the picture shows that Frets can't hold (a
  second part, fingerings) and what you did with it.


11. FROM SHEET MUSIC (STANDARD NOTATION, NO TAB)
------------------------------------------------

The score gives the pitches; you choose where each is played. Guitar
music is written an octave above how it sounds, and Frets' staff is drawn
the same way, so a note on Frets' staff lands where it is on the page.

- Find the places a written note can be played: in standard tuning the
  open strings are written E3 A3 D4 G4 B4 E5 (strings 6 to 1; middle C is
  C4), and a fret is the number of semitones above its string's open
  note. Written G4 is 0.3, 5.4, 10.5 or 15.6. For another \tuning, write
  each open string an octave above its tuning note. For a guitar tuned
  down with all six strings alike, the score is written as fingered: look
  the notes up in standard tuning.
- Choose a position, not note by note: keep a phrase within about four
  frets (a finger a fret), as low on the neck as covers it. Use open
  strings in open-position keys (E, A, D, G, C) unless the music is high
  or marked otherwise. Change position at a rest, a long note or a slide,
  never in the middle of a fast run.
- Follow the score's own fingering: a circled number is the string
  (circled 3 is string 3), a Roman numeral (V, VII) the fret the hand
  sits at, "sul G" string 3. Small numbers 1 to 4 by the notes are
  left-hand fingers: they confirm a position but aren't written.
- Chords: one note a string, on neighbouring strings, in a shape a hand
  can hold (at most four fretted notes across about four frets, or a
  barre).
- Marks: a slur between two different notes on one string is h on the
  first; a straight line or "gliss." between them sl; a tie "-" on the
  second; "8va" over the staff is ot 8va on each beat under it, with frets
  an octave above the written notes; diamond heads nh; staccato st;
  accents ac and hac; a trill tr (kept, not drawn).
- Key and time: the key signature is \ks (major or minor as the music
  reads, \ks Eminor), the time signature \ts; an accidental lasts to the
  end of its bar.
- Two voices (stems up and stems down at once): Frets holds one. Keep the
  melody; where a bass note starts with a melody note, put both in one
  chord with the melody's length, and give a held bass as ties. Say that
  you did this.
- When done, tell the user to open Notation, then Staff or Both, and
  compare the staff with the printed page.
